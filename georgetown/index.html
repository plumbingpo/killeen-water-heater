How Georgetown Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.

Hot Water Headaches Georgetown Homeowners Face

Every type of water heater fails in its own way, and we work on all of them in Georgetown. Gas tanks usually give trouble through thermocouples and flame sensors, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric tanks burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
