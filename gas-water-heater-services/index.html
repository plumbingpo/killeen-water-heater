No hot water from a gas heater usually means one thing: the burner is not firing, and something told it not to. Gas heaters are built around safety. The control valve will not open unless a thermocouple or flame sensor proves there is a flame, and many models lock out if the flame arrestor screen at the bottom gets clogged or the heater senses spilled exhaust. So the real question is which safety did its job, and why. Here in Killeen, TX, the answer is most often a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot or burner, a lint-choked air intake, a failing gas valve, or a vent that is no longer drafting properly.

Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • Rust-colored hot water or rust streaks on the tank
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • A pilot that lights, then goes out when you release the knob

A Slow Leak Never Stays Slow

Tanks rarely fail all at once. They corrode from the inside for months, and the warning signs show up long before the floor gets wet. At the first hint of a problem, reach out to Killeen Water Heater at (254) 223-6498 and we'll take a look.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Gas water heaters are among the riskiest appliances to work on without training, because the biggest hazards are invisible. A gas connection that is not quite tight can leak slowly for days. A vent connector that has lost its slope, or a flue partly blocked by debris, can let carbon monoxide spill into the house every time the burner fires, with no smell to warn you. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically require a permit and an inspection. Relighting a pilot by following the label is something most homeowners can do; almost everything past that deserves a technician.

Professional service gets you a correct answer and a safe appliance. We confirm thermocouple output, gas pressure, burner flame, and draft with real instruments, not trial and error. Every gas connection we make is tested for leaks before we leave, and vent connectors are secured with the right slope and clearances. When a replacement is needed, we size the new heater by first-hour rating, handle the permit where one is required, and add the expansion tank, sediment trap, drain pan, or seismic straps your home needs. The result is hot water you do not have to worry about.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
